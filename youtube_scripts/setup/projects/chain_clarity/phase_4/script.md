## SECTION 2 — HOT WALLETS VS COLD WALLETS (2:00–4:00)

[TITLE CARD: "02 · HOT VS COLD"]
[B-ROLL: MetaMask on a laptop screen, Ledger hardware device, safe deposit box]

SPOKEN:
"There are two categories of wallet: hot and cold.

A hot wallet is software connected to the internet.
MetaMask. Phantom. Trust Wallet.
Convenient for daily transactions.
Exposed to online threats at all times.

A cold wallet is hardware that stores your private key offline.
Ledger. Trezor. Coldcard.
Never connected to the internet unless you are actively signing.
The private key never leaves the device.

Think of it like this.
Your hot wallet is your physical wallet — what you carry for coffee money.
Your cold wallet is a safe deposit box — where you keep the serious funds.

A reasonable split for most people:
Small amounts for active use go on a hot wallet.
Significant holdings go on hardware.

The device itself is not the asset.
The seed phrase is the asset."

## SECTION 3 — THE SEED PHRASE (4:00–6:00)

[TITLE CARD: "03 · THE SEED PHRASE"]
[B-ROLL: 12-word mnemonic displayed on hardware screen, metal backup plates]

SPOKEN:
"Your seed phrase is a sequence of 12 or 24 common English words.
It is generated once, when you set up your wallet.
It is a human-readable encoding of your private key.

If your hardware wallet breaks — you restore from the seed phrase.
If you buy a new device — you restore from the seed phrase.
If someone finds your seed phrase — they own your funds. Permanently.

The seed phrase is the single most important piece of data you will generate.
Treat it accordingly.

Where should you store it?
Not in a photo on your phone.
Not in a note-taking app.
Not in a cloud document.
Not emailed to yourself.

The gold standard is a stamped metal backup.
Metal does not burn. Metal does not flood. Metal does not corrupt.
Write your seed phrase on metal, store it somewhere physically secure, and tell no one.

One copy is a single point of failure.
Two copies in separate secure locations is the minimum standard."

## SECTION 4 — TOP 5 ATTACK VECTORS (6:00–9:00)

[TITLE CARD: "04 · HOW THEY STEAL IT"]
[B-ROLL: Phishing email screenshot, fake MetaMask extension, SIM swap SMS]

SPOKEN:
"Here are the five most common ways crypto gets stolen.

Attack one: phishing links.
A fake website that looks like your wallet or exchange.
You enter your seed phrase. It is captured instantly.
The rule: you never type your seed phrase into a website. Ever.
Hardware wallets show the seed phrase only on the device screen.

Attack two: malicious wallet extensions.
A browser extension that mimics MetaMask.
It intercepts your transactions and redirects funds.
Always download wallet software from the official source only.
Check the URL. Check the developer. Check the review count.

Attack three: SIM swapping.
An attacker calls your mobile carrier, impersonates you, and transfers your number.
Now they receive your SMS two-factor codes.
Use an authenticator app — not SMS — for all exchange accounts.
Hardware security keys like YubiKey are even stronger.

Attack four: fake support scams.
Someone in a Discord or Telegram pretends to be support staff.
They ask for your seed phrase to 'troubleshoot' your wallet.
No legitimate protocol ever asks for your seed phrase. Ever.

Attack five: clipboard hijacking malware.
You copy a wallet address to send funds.
Malware replaces the clipboard address with the attacker's address.
Always verify the first four and last four characters of a destination address before confirming."

## SECTION 5 — YOUR SECURITY CHECKLIST (9:00–10:30)

[TITLE CARD: "05 · YOUR SECURITY CHECKLIST"]
[B-ROLL: Checklist animation, hardware wallet unboxing, YubiKey]

SPOKEN:
"Here is the actionable checklist.

One: Move significant funds off exchanges into self-custody.
Two: Buy hardware from the manufacturer's official website only.
Three: Generate your seed phrase on the device — never online.
Four: Write your seed phrase on metal and store it physically.
Five: Never share your seed phrase with anyone, for any reason.
Six: Use an authenticator app for all exchange accounts — remove SMS two-factor.
Seven: Before any transaction, verify destination addresses character by character.
Eight: Use a dedicated device for large transactions where possible.
Nine: Bookmark every wallet or exchange URL — never search for them.
Ten: Review and revoke unused DeFi contract approvals quarterly.

Security is not a product you buy.
It is a practice you build."
